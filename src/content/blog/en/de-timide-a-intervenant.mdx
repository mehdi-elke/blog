Since I was a child, I have always been very shy. I've always found it difficult to express myself in front of strangers, and the professional world didn't help much at first.

Yet, a few years ago, when the opportunity to teach as a guest lecturer arose, I jumped at the chance to step out of my comfort zone.

Here, I’m sharing my method for creating a training course.

*Disclaimer: I am not a pedagogical expert, but a tech enthusiast who loves learning and sharing.*

## The Objective Above All

Everything starts with a subject and an objective for a specific audience: learning the basics of Java for students, or developing production-ready APIs for junior developers.

From there, I extract the knowledge (the theory) and the skills (the know-how).

Knowledge is the recipe; skill is the cooked dish.

| Learning Object | Knowledge (Theory) | Skill (Practice) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arrays** | Knowing that an array has a fixed size and starts at index 0. | Successfully reversing the elements of an array without creating a new one. |
| **Inheritance** | Explaining the `extends` keyword and the "is a" relationship. | Creating a consistent class hierarchy. |

## Structure and Evaluation

I use a mindmap to throw out my ideas without constraints, then I categorize them by level (beginner, intermediate, advanced). At this stage, I already define the evaluation criteria: how do I validate that the material is acquired?

<Image src={mindmap} alt="My course structure mindmap" class="my-8 border border-border-main" />

- **Knowledge:** a direct question.
- **Skill:** a lab exercise (TP).

## The "Doc-as-code" Approach

For my materials, I use Marp (and soon Asciidoctor). I write in Markdown, and the tool generates HTML or PDF.

<Image src={marpapp} alt="Marp interface for slides" class="my-8 border border-border-main" />

Why?
- History tracking on Git.
- Focusing on content rather than form during feedback.
- Using AI to help me correct or generate the structure.

Warning: You must remain the thinking head. You do the thinking, the AI does the execution.

## The "Fil Rouge" (Capstone) Project

To stay practical, I rely on a project that progresses alongside the theory. For Spring Boot, for example, this allows me to offer "forks" based on the students' levels: the fastest can add Kafka or an AI, while others consolidate the basics.

My goal: to stick to reality. I provide my assignments in the form of **User Stories** and introduce concepts of observability or testing, which are essential in the industry.

## The Feedback Loop (Even When It Hurts)

One last element that seems important to me is the feedback loop. In 6 and a half years of teaching, I've been lucky enough to collect a vast majority of maximum scores (10/10). It's rewarding, but that's not what makes you progress the most.

<Image src={feedback} alt="Student feedback contrast" class="my-8 border border-border-main" />

Paradoxically, it's the one and only '0' I ever received that left the biggest mark on me. I never found out why, and it was a real lesson: learning to filter through feedback.

- **Organization:** (rooms, schedules) I can't do anything about it.
- **Materials:** I lighten them up if I'm told I "read too much" from my slides.
- **Pedagogy:** this is where I progress the most.

## Teaching in the Age of AI

Today, it is impossible to ignore ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude. To be certain that concepts are understood, I now prefer paper questionnaires for knowledge—a 20 or 30-minute quiz makes it much easier to verify that the knowledge is actually there.
And for the practical part, in my opinion, nothing beats discussions and presentations.

## How to Get Started?

If you're tempted by the adventure, don't stay in your corner. The hardest part is often finding the first door in.

- **Networking:** Talk about it on LinkedIn or to your former professors. Schools are constantly looking for expert profiles for their specializations.
- **Contact Pedagogical Managers:** Don't hesitate to send your CV directly to schools in your area. They are often in a rush to find instructors for specific technologies (Cloud, AI, DevOps).
- **Platforms:** Solutions like [Tenors](https://tenors.fr) exist to centralize teaching offers and facilitate contact with establishments.

## Conclusion

Teaching forces me to stay up to date and to dig deeper into subjects thanks to the students' insightful questions. It's also a chance to pass on our industry's best practices to future colleagues.

If I had one piece of advice: **try it at least once, you'll see how much you can get out of it!**
